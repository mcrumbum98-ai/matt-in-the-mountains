Taking the Scenic Route

Hiking Mt. Colden via the Trap Dike
Adventures · Hiking · Adirondack High Peaks · July 25, 2026

A Test of Grit and Mental Fortitude

The day started out with a sleepy dawn drive to the Upper Works trail head near the small town of Newcomb, New York. Jake, Willy and I took a moment don our gear and collect ourselves before beginning the leisurely stroll past the decaying remnants of the mining boom town of Adirondac or Tahawus (a Seneca word meaning “cloud-splitter”, later applied in reference to Mount Marcy). From there the trail slowly transitions from a wide flat path to a narrow trail that time and nature is slowly reclaiming. We briefly stopped to visit the monument to David Henderson on the shores of Calamity Pond before moving on to the Flowed Lands and past the dam at the end of Lake Colden. We continued on past the devastation of the recent Jackson slide that tore though the area in the summer of 2025. I took a moment to take in the awe of the power of nature but to also pay some respects to the man the slide was dedicated to, Brendan Jackson, the assistant DEC forest ranger whom I had shared drinks and conversation with from time to time while living in Saranac Lake, NY.

Shortly after we passed a friendly older couple that asked where we were headed. After informing her we were aiming to summit Colden (after we had already passed the southern approach) she promptly said “WOW! Youre taking the long way around.“ and I understood that she was thinking “You idiots missed the turn.“ What she did not know was that we were not taking the long route, but the scenic route… We were about to attempt one of the most dangerous hikes in the Northeastern US.

The Trap Dike

After reaching Avalanche Lake, we departed the beaten path and began somewhat of a bushwhack towards my fuzzy idea of where the spit of debris at the foot of the feature called the Trap Dike. It is best described as a slide mixed with a couloir. The slides at the top feed into a steep narrow gully that formed over time from a dike, or soft igneous intrusion in the surrounding anorthosite bedrock.

To call the Trap Dike “steep“ is an understatement. Over a horizontal distance between the base of the Trap Dike to the Summit of about a mile and a half, you climb roughly 1,800 feet. Many people choose to do this route with ropes and trad climbing equipment. Within the couloir section there are two main waterfall features. We happened to do this hike in a fantastic weather window of almost two weeks straight of no rain, so the water was a minimal hazard. The second waterfall “crux“ however was incredibly technical and required some serious focus and a fair bit of ass-clenching.

The Slides

For the most part aside from the crux and a few other steep scrambles the Trap Dike was not too technical, just physically tiring. Eventually the steep rock scrambles transitioned to slightly gentler slopes of slide debris until we reached the end of the Trap Dike. But the hard part had just begun… the Trap Dike spits you out at the bottom of slide created in 2011 during hurricane Irene. We started up this slide and then traversed/bushwhacked over to the older 1869 slide. These were the scariest parts to me. Give me a steep cliff with good holds all day long but any time we stopped on the smooth, steep rock face all I could think about was slipping and cheese-grating roughly 100yards before falling to my death.

The Summit

We met a group of nice French Canadians that we followed for the remainder of the dizzying climb up the slide and through a short bushwhack to the summit.

After a short lunch we began the long march down and back to the car. It was at this point all of our bodies began to feel the strain we had just put on them. Willy started to get a bad case of patellar inflammation, I had some IT band pain on my right knee and Jake… well Jake could have used some body glide but we had to make do with a walking stick for Willy and a bottle of Neosporin for Jake.

After a short stop at the Lake Colden Dam to filter some water with our Sawer Squeeze water filters, we continued on for the last few grueling miles back to Tahawus and the car. Total trip of 16 miles, 4068ft of elevation gain, 7 hours 46 minutes moving time and a lifetime of memories.
